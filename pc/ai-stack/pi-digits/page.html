Check a sample yourself

Your browser re-does the fingerprint math: the block (with its position) is hashed, walked up its proof, and must land on the published root. Then the block is compared with the stored digits at that position. Change one digit and it fails. (The samples and proofs are built from the regenerated digits; nothing extra is stored.)

The equation

This is everything that is stored. The equation (recipe) is written once; each run adds 40 bytes: its size and the Merkle root of its digits. One Ed25519 signature covers all of it, and one OpenTimestamps proof dates it in Bitcoin. Follow the equation and you get the digits back; they count only if they land on the stored roots.

Turning by one radian never repeats

A full turn is 2π. Step around the circle by exactly 1 radian each time: because π is irrational the point never lands on a spot it has hit before, and the marks fill the circle evenly. A rational turn (switch it) closes up and repeats.

π's digits as a woven circle

Digits 0–9 sit around the circle; a thread runs from each digit of π to the next. The weave comes out even because π's digits behave like random ones. This draws π; it cannot compute it.
